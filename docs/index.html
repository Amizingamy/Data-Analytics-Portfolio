Case study

Did the Brew Room Christmas campaign work?

I ran this campaign at a café in Mylapore, Chennai. Here I measure the revenue lift, the products behind it, the Instagram effect and loyalty behaviour.

About the data. The real sales figures are confidential, so the dataset is simulated around the few real numbers I can share (about ₹5,00,000 from hampers, about 20 loyalty sign-ups, about 6,000 organic reach). Read the results as a demonstration of method, not as the business's real results.

Daily revenue, 1 to 25 December

Revenue jumped from the day the campaign started on 13 December.

Campaign revenue by product

Gifting hampers were about half of it.

Average reach per Instagram post

Riddle posts reached about 58% more people.

Average spend per loyalty member (₹)

Higher tiers spend more. Referrers spend more too.

Customers vs revenue by RFM segment (%)

A quarter of customers bring over half the revenue.

Is the lift real? Average daily revenue rose from ₹20,897 to ₹79,214 (+279%, 95% bootstrap range 222% to 344%). Welch t-test p = 1.1e-07 and Mann-Whitney p = 1.2e-05. Caveat: December demand rises anyway, so with real data I would add a control to separate the campaign from the season. Read the full notebook.

Projects from SPJain Global. The Ve-Glam dataset is synthetic, so read it as a demonstration of method.

Ve-Glam: customer segmentation for a vegan cosmetics e-commerce platform

Data Analytics, SPJain Global, individual project. Tools: Python (to generate and clean the data), JMP

  • Set two business goals: raise awareness by 15% and raise sales by 20% through better product recommendations and less cart abandonment.
  • Built a synthetic customer dataset for three UAE emirates (Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah), then ran RFM analysis, association rule mining and K-means clustering (4 segments).
  • Turned the segments into actions: premium bundles for infrequent high spenders, loyalty rewards for frequent low spenders, education content and influencer reviews for researchers, budget offers for price-sensitive buyers.
  • Used cart-abandonment, seasonality and brand-by-location charts to decide when and where to run offers.

Dissertation: Gen Z's ethical views on Google's AI-powered ads and consumer neuroscience

International Business Research, SPJain Global, 2023 to 2024. Singapore and Dubai

  • Surveyed 315 Gen Z consumers (232 in Dubai, 83 in Singapore) and interviewed 20 consumers plus industry experts in marketing.
  • Tested hypotheses with descriptive statistics, t-tests, ANOVA and multiple regression. Awareness differed significantly between Singapore and Dubai (t = 2.76, p = 0.006), and perceptions varied with how much people value ethical standards (ANOVA F = 3.51, p = 0.008).
  • Found high awareness of AI-driven neuromarketing (mean 7.22 out of 9) alongside privacy and trust concerns, and recommended a balanced, transparent approach.
