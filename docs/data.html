Did the campaign actually work?

A case study from the Brew Room Christmas campaign I ran in Chennai: SQL, Python and a dashboard to measure what changed and why.

About the data. The real sales figures are confidential, so the dataset is simulated around the few real numbers I can share (about ₹5,00,000 from hampers, about 20 loyalty sign-ups, about 6,000 organic reach). The real campaign ran at Christmas 2022; the calendar dates in the simulated file are placeholders. Read the results as a demonstration of method, not as the café's real results.

Daily revenue, 1 to 25 December

Revenue jumped from the day the campaign started on 13 December.

Gifting hampers were about half of it.

Average spend per loyalty member (₹)

Higher tiers spend more. Referrers spend more too.

Customers vs revenue by RFM segment (%)

A quarter of customers bring over half the revenue.

Is the lift real? Average daily revenue rose from ₹20,897 to ₹79,214 (+279%, 95% bootstrap range 222% to 344%). Welch t-test p = 1.1e-07 and Mann-Whitney p = 1.2e-05. Caveat: December demand rises anyway, so with real data I would add a control to separate the campaign from the season.

  • Two goals: raise awareness by 15% and sales by 20% through better recommendations and less cart abandonment.

Dissertation: Gen Z and AI-powered ads

International Business Research, SPJain Global, 2023 to 2024

  • Surveyed 315 Gen Z consumers (232 Dubai, 83 Singapore) and interviewed 20 consumers and marketing experts.
  • t-tests, ANOVA and regression: awareness differed between Singapore and Dubai (t = 2.76, p = 0.006).
  • High awareness of AI-driven neuromarketing (7.22 out of 9) alongside privacy and trust concerns.
